# Milestone 4: Original skin with fast loading (change note)

> **Reading this in the repository:** this note was written beside the local review copy. The folders it mentions (`change/`, `review-evidence/`, `.backup/`, `Desktop\PainMapper-m4-loading`) hold the patch file, the test harness, timings, pictures and checkpoints on the PC where the work was done; they are not part of this repository. The code it describes is.

**Status (19 November 2026):** prepared and checked in an isolated copy (`Desktop\PainMapper-m4-loading`, port 5176), then, on the designer's instruction, **applied to Milestone 4** (`Desktop\PainMapper-main`, port 5173) in two steps: the Skin switch with the fast loading, then the ••• menu entry for it (section 9). Milestone 4's source is now identical to this copy's. Not reviewed by Eddie. Nothing published or pushed. The anatomy pilot and the focus-reveal work copy are unchanged. "Milestone 4" in the comparisons below means Milestone 4 as it was before this change.

## 1. In short

This change brings one thing from the pilots into Milestone 4: a **Skin switch, "Gray | Original"**, with the fast loading that was developed in the focus-reveal work copy. Gray is Milestone 4's normal clay look and is where every visit starts. Original shows the mannequin's own skin, hair and eyes. Nothing else from the pilots is included: no anatomy reveal, no pain-intensity colour, no pilot panel.

What the fast loading means for a learner:

- The first switch to Original no longer downloads the 54 MB model again or reads it a second time, and the page does not freeze while it is prepared.
- The body stays Gray until Original is completely ready, then changes in one step. Meanwhile the Original button shows a small spinner, and if it takes more than a moment, a short note says what is happening.
- If preparing fails, a short message says so, with "Try again"; the body stays Gray.
- Choosing Gray always wins, at any moment.
- Location, side, case details, conditions and the camera never change when the skin changes. The choice is not saved.
- The same choice is also in the header's ••• menu ("Show original skin" / "Show gray skin"), so it can be reached on small phones where the corner switch has no room.

**Result on this PC (production build):** Original appears about 0.39 s after the press instead of 0.78–0.81 s, the longest frame is about 60 ms instead of 0.48 s, and nothing is downloaded again (the pilot downloaded the 54 MB model a second time). Start-up is about 0.08 s faster than Milestone 4's. Gray looks exactly like Milestone 4. All automated checks pass, Milestone 4's own checks pass on the copy and on Milestone 4 after applying, and three rounds of independent review (plus one more of the menu entry) left nothing to fix beyond the limits in section 7. On 19 November the same builds measured about 0.44–0.46 s to Original; the PC was slower that day for the build with and without the menu entry alike (section 6).

## 2. What a learner sees

- **Where:** bottom-right corner of the 3D view, under the zoom buttons ("Skin  Gray | Original"). On a phone-sized screen, just above the panel on the right, moving with the panel; hidden while the panel covers the view or a detail editor is open, and, like Milestone 4's small body figure above the panel, it steps aside whenever the uncovered view is too short for it to stay clear of the side labels, Front | Back and Whole body (it comes back when the panel is lowered). On landscape phones it sits left of the zoom row. This is placement **option A** from the interface review (see section 8).
- **First time Original is chosen:** the button turns on at once and shows a spinner; after 150 ms a small note appears beside the control: "Preparing the original skin: decoding its textures… / preparing its shading… / sending it to the graphics card… / showing it…", with a thin progress bar (a real fraction only while sending to the graphics card). The body stays Gray until the switch. Screen readers hear "Preparing the original skin", then "Original skin shown".
- **Coming back to Original later:** about a tenth of a second (no note appears). After a minute in Gray the graphics-card copies of the Original-only textures are released to save memory; the next switch then only sends them again (the decoded pictures are kept).
- **Hovering over Original** (or tabbing to it) already starts decoding in the background; nothing changes on screen.
- **Failure:** "The original skin couldn’t be prepared. The body stays gray." with a "Try again" button, and a small alert mark on Original; screen readers hear the message at once, and Original and Try again carry it as their description. If a retry fails again, keyboard focus moves to "Try again" (unless the learner has moved on to something else meanwhile). Pressing Original again also retries.
- **Where there is little room:** the "Preparing" note gives way first (the spinner stays). On phones where the uncovered view is short, and on landscape phones, the failure note keeps only its "Try again" button, right above the pill, so it stays clear of the selected location in the middle of the view; on the smallest landscape phones even that does not fit, and the mark on Original stands in for it. While a note shows on narrower desktop windows and landscape phones, the small corner body figure makes way.
- **In the ••• menu** (added after the first review of this note, placement options A and C together): one entry in its own section between the case actions and About, named for what it will do: "Show original skin" when the body is Gray, "Show gray skin" when Original is chosen (its small print says "Preparing the original skin…" until it is ready). It does exactly what the corner switch does, and the two always agree. After a failed preparation there are two entries: "Keep gray skin" and "Try original skin again". When Original is chosen from the menu, the ••• button itself carries a small turning ring while it is prepared and a red dot if it failed (on the smallest phones that is the only sign on screen, because the corner switch is not there); the button's spoken name says the same. The entry is greyed out, with "When the 3D patient is showing", until the 3D patient has loaded and while the phone panel covers it completely. Opening the menu prepares nothing; resting the mouse on the entry starts decoding in the background, as on the corner switch. A return to Gray is now also spoken ("Gray skin shown"). On very short screens (a phone on its side) the menu scrolls instead of running off the bottom (going by its measured size, Milestone 4's four-entry menu was already taller than the shortest of those screens).
- **Milestone 4's drag hint** ("Drag to rotate · Scroll to zoom", shown until the first drag): unchanged on large screens. On desktop windows narrower than about 1,400 px it is centred in the space left of the Skin control and wraps there if needed (on the narrowest, a little smaller and without its icon), instead of running into the control; it also makes way while the skin is being prepared or has failed.

## 4. How it works (for the reviewer)

1. **Start-up read** (`modelLoader.js`). Before the model is parsed, the GLB's JSON chunk is rewritten in place (same length, padded with spaces) without the texture slots only Original uses, so the clay's 8 textures are decoded and the 27 Original-only ones are not. The full material list is kept in `gltf.userData.pmOriginalMaterials`. If anything about the file is unexpected, the file is read unchanged, exactly as Milestone 4 does.
2. **Decoding** (`originalSkin.js`). The Original materials are created by the same glTF parser from the same read (no download, no geometry parsed again); their textures are decoded off the main thread, a few materials' worth per frame, then the materials are made in the file's order (so the picture is the same on every visit).
3. **Compiling.** The shaders of the meshes in view are compiled with `renderer.compileAsync`, a few per frame (parallel compilation where the browser has it).
4. **Uploading.** Each in-view mesh is drawn once into a single hidden pixel (scissored, one mesh per frame), which sends its textures to the graphics card and makes the browser finish its shader for that mesh's exact vertex format. On Windows this second part is what froze the first Original frame (Direct3D through ANGLE). three.js's shader-error check is skipped during these draws (it would make the page wait) and done right afterwards (`LINK_STATUS`). Meshes that came into view meanwhile (Whole body, Centre, a drag) are prepared before the switch too.
5. **Showing.** The materials are switched; the status turns ready after the first Original frame.
6. **Afterwards, in the background:** the meshes outside the view get the same treatment, one texture or one mesh per frame, so a later zoom-out does not stall.
7. **Back to Gray:** instant; after 60 s in Gray the Original-only textures are released from the graphics card (never the clay's).
8. **Safety:** a request counter makes the latest choice win at every step; each step has a 20 s limit (counting only time the page is visible) and ends in the error state, never an endless "Preparing"; an error or Gray leaves the body Gray.
9. **Graphics context lost and restored** (a GPU reset, or a phone browser dropping it in the background): "already prepared" is remembered per graphics state (three.js's `renderer.properties`, which it replaces on a restore), so the next switch to Original prepares everything again instead of freezing. If it happens in the middle of a preparation, the preparation ends in the failure state (body Gray) rather than switch to something unprepared, and "Try again" prepares for the new state.
10. **The 3D view reloads the model** (its error screen's "Try again"): everything made for the old model and renderer is forgotten (`forgetOriginalModel`), so neither stays in memory.
11. **Two places offer the choice** (`skinChoice.js`): which skin is asked for lives in a small store outside the components, read by the 3D view (`ViewerPanel`) and by the header (`AppHeader`, through `useSkinMenu`). The rest of the app, including the panel and its list of conditions, does not re-render when the skin changes, and the header reads only the preparation's phase, not its progress steps. The store also notes whether Original was last asked for from the menu (only then does the ••• button show the ring or the dot; the corner control shows its own progress), whether the 3D patient is ready and in view, and whether the skin has been changed since the model loaded (so "Gray skin shown" is spoken on a return to Gray, but not for the Gray every load starts with).

## 7. Limits and things to know

1. **One fast PC only.** Everything was measured on this PC (Windows 11, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 SUPER, Chrome and Edge 157). Slower computers will take longer. The phone results are desktop emulation; nothing was tried on a physical phone, on Safari (iPhone, iPad) or on Firefox.
2. **More graphics memory right after the first Original:** about 113 MB more than the pilots' first version (448 vs 335 MB here), because the rest of the body is prepared in the background right after the switch. That is what avoids a freeze at the first zoom-out (measured above). After a minute back in Gray, the 27 Original-only textures are released; 4 small textures (about 1 MB) that three.js makes for the eye layers' shape blending stay until the page is closed.
3. **One short hitch remains:** a single frame of about 60 ms, about 0.2 s after the press, while one mesh's large textures are sent to the graphics card. It could be spread over several frames, which would make Original appear a little later (roughly 0.05–0.2 s, depending on the screen's refresh rate). Not done here, so the change stays identical to the reviewed focus-reveal pipeline.
4. **Original against the pilots' pictures:** a few hair and eyelash edge pixels differ from the anatomy pilot (section 5); between visits the copy's picture differs at most in a few dozen hair-edge pixels by a few colour levels.
5. **The Original look shows the model file's own textures.** Milestone 4 already downloads them with the model; the open question about the mannequin's licence (anatomy pilot report) applies to showing them.
6. **Small phones:** where the panel leaves too little of the view uncovered, the corner switch steps aside rather than cover Milestone 4's labels or buttons. On a phone the size of an iPhone SE in Safari (375×548) that means it is there until a location is chosen, but not while one is shown (the panel then leaves too little room). The ••• menu entry closes that gap: both skins can be chosen there at any time the body is in view.
